# 0015: Improvements ship behind flags flagd serves from a namespace of its own, judged on the traffic generator's figures

## Decision

An improvement Martin asks for ships in the app switched off, behind a feature flag its spec names, and he widens the flag a stage at a time from the console: Off, Checks, Preview, Share and Everyone ([feature flags](../../COMPONENTS/FEATURE-FLAGS.md#the-stages)). Five choices hold it together:

- **flagd runs alone in `flagd`, watching one `FeatureFlag`.** The state of every flag is `flags` in `flagd`, which flagd reads through the Kubernetes API with a Role that may get, list and watch `featureflags` there and nothing else. Only the kind's definition is installed, not OpenFeature's operator. Git holds `flags` with no flags, and Argo CD makes it and leaves its flags alone. Of the factory's services, only the admin service writes it, by name, and admission holds each of its writes to the stages' shapes that `policy/flags.ts` renders, keeps `flags` the only `FeatureFlag` there, and refuses its deletion. flagd's image is admitted by digest in a copy of admission control of its own, `flagd-images`.
- **The app evaluates in-process, and fails off.** The app's flag context, a module code-owned in the app's repository and the only one there that may import OpenFeature or flagd's provider, streams the flag set from flagd's sync port and evaluates each request itself. A flag is on only when flagd says so, and every flag turns off once the app has been without flagd for a minute, or two when its connection died silently ([failing off](../../COMPONENTS/FEATURE-FLAGS.md#failing-off)).
- **The mark is kept by the network.** Checks is on for requests that carry `x-unmeasured`, the header the factory's own checks already send. Every route to the app passes through the one Middleware that removes it, which admission fixes (`website-mark`), and only the pods the app's network policy names reach the app any other way ([the mark](../../COMPONENTS/ADMISSION-CONTROL.md#the-mark)). The mark carries no secret.
- **The share is the traffic generator's signed ids.** At Share a flag is on for 10, 25 or 50% of the generator's requests, chosen by flagd's `fractional` rule over the id each carries. Each id is signed with Ed25519 over the host, method, path and time, with a private key only the generator holds; the app holds the public key, in the ConfigMap `share-key`, which only `make share-key`'s user may write, and treats an id as signed only for its own request and its minute ([the share's signed ids](../../COMPONENTS/FEATURE-FLAGS.md#the-shares-signed-ids)). No visitor is ever in a share.
- **Verify judges a share on the generator's own figures.** The generator records each request's status and time by id, as metrics of its own. Verify works out each id's side with flagd's evaluation library, at the version the app's lockfile pins, and compares the two sides on errors and latency with the canary's thresholds, over at least half an hour from a minute after Martin's move, setting aside any time a canary was in flight ([Share](../../COMPONENTS/RELEASE-AND-VERIFY.md#share)).

## Why

- **flagd beside the app would hand the app's repository a way in.** Agents write the app, and its repository may make objects in `website`. flagd in the app's pod, or beside it in `website`, would put a token that reads the flags, or the flags themselves, within reach of a change to the app. In `flagd`, the app's pods hold no token and no route to the Kubernetes API, and reach flagd's sync port alone.
- **A resource, not a file in Git.** A flag's stage is production state that Martin sets in seconds, without a deploy: the kill switch is worth having only if it needs no pull request. In Git, every merge would put each flag back to how Git has it. A `FeatureFlag` is something Kubernetes can guard: a Role names who may write it, and admission holds what they write to a fixed set of shapes, so the admin service, or anyone holding its token, can stage a flag and nothing more. The operator would add a controller with wide rights to do what one watch already does.
- **In-process, so no request waits on flagd,** and flagd going away cannot slow the shop. But the provider keeps serving the last flag set it had, so without the minute a kill switch would never reach an app cut off from flagd. Failing off chooses the old behaviour over the new while flagd is away: every improvement goes dark together, and the shop is as it was before any of them.
- **The network, not a key, for the mark.** A key in the probes would be a secret for every check, in every place they run, and the journeys gate runs them in GitHub's runners. The header already kept the factory's checks out of the figures a canary is judged on, and the front door already stands between visitors and the app, so stripping it there and fencing every other way in costs one Middleware, one admission rule and the network policy the app had.
- **The generator's requests, signed, rather than a share of visitors.** Putting a person on one side of a share and keeping them there would mean an id on their device, which the app does not keep (only the preview cookie of one who asks for it). The generator walks every page all the time, so its requests are a fair sample of both sides; its ids turn independently of the canary's split. Unsigned, anyone could send an id and walk into a share; signed with a key only the generator holds, the app can check a signature but never make one, and a captured one is good for one page for a minute.
- **The generator's figures, not the app's.** The code being judged must not mark its own work: the app could label a request either side, or send the collector any metric it likes. The generator measures what it sent and what came back, and Verify reads only the series the collector stamped as from the generator's pod (`provenance.ts`).
- **flagd's library, not flagd's answer.** Verify must put each id on the side the app put it. The app evaluates with `@openfeature/flagd-core`; flagd's own evaluation service is another implementation of the `fractional` rule, which a flagd release may change without the app's library (flagd 0.17's put 15 to 49 of the 101 ids on another side; 0.18's agrees). So Verify evaluates with the same library at the same version, and a recorded test (`scripts/flagd-sides.ts`, `apps/factory/test/line/flagd-sides.json`) holds it to the app's sides.
- **A canary and a share take turns.** Split by version and by flag at once, each comparison can blame the wrong one: a slow flag on more of a canary's requests would roll back a good release. So Share is refused while a canary is in flight, and a canary that starts during one is set aside from the comparison; a release never waits for a flag.

## Consequences

- A flag set by hand outside the stages' shapes is dropped, and so turned off, at the admin service's next write: only the admin service's writes are held to the shapes, and an administrator's are not.
- flagd's readiness follows its first read, not its watch, so a watch broken after start would leave the app holding old flags while flagd looks healthy. The admin service asks each of the app's pods every minute for the revision of the flag set it holds (`x-flags-revision`, told only to a marked request) and compares it with `flags`; Martin's phone hears when they differ for five minutes, or for a minute after a narrowing or Off ([whether the app holds the flags](../../COMPONENTS/ADMIN-SERVICE.md#whether-the-app-holds-the-flags)). The check believes what the app says, which its code-owned flag context tells.
- The app's flag context decides who is a check, a previewer or in a share, so a change to it waits for Martin's approval in the app's repository, and no other module there may ask flagd anything.
- The factory's flagd-core is pinned exactly to the app's. When the app's provider moves, the factory's library, and the record of each id's side, move in the same release, or Verify's sides may not be the app's.
- `share-key` is written only from the Mac, by `make share-key`, not by Argo CD. A cluster made before the key has none, and until it does the generator sends no id, the app treats nothing as signed, and no comparison at Share can be judged.
- The comparison needs the generator running: without its requests, or without Prometheus, a flag waits at Share, and Everyone waits for its comparison.
